Decision models are API endpoints that receive a text and a list of options and return a probability for each option. We compare
three of them (OpenAI Decisions, Base Labs Elephant-1 and Mammoth-1), used zero-shot, with compact classifiers trained on 5, 20 or all
labelled examples per class (TF-IDF, SetFit, mmBERT, Qwen3 0.6B--8B with LoRA/QLoRA) on Banking77, Italian MASSIVE and a synthetic Italian
customer-service task, using paired bootstrap tests, three seeds and consumer-grade GPUs. With label names only, so with no labelled
example of the task, the best decision model was 0.21--0.24 macro-F1 ahead of the best compact classifier trained on 5 examples per
class. With the full training sets the gap shrank to 0.024 on Banking77 and 0.055 on Italian MASSIVE, and was not resolved on the synthetic
task. On Banking77 the recipe of an earlier exploratory study raised Qwen3-0.6B from 0.910 to 0.936, and its difference from Mammoth-1 was
no longer resolved: with many labels, the comparison depends on how the small model is trained. Elephant-1 was only 0.013 ahead of its own
base checkpoint, Qwen3.5-9B, used zero-shot on the synthetic task. Category descriptions changed results in different directions for
different models and datasets. Code, protocol, synthetic data and per-example predictions are released.
